# What the best short, cheap incrementals do right

*Topic study, 2026-10-21. Question from the owner: read the top 5 to 10 short, cheap, highly rated incrementals (Shelldiver, Nodebuster, Berry Bury Berry,
Digseum and similar) and summarise what they do right. Read for **12 games**: the Steam record, the all-language review summary, the weekly review histogram,
the full news feed, and **about 250 to 500 English positive and 30 to 270 English negative reviews each** (keyword counts, approximate; the review samples
contain some duplicate pages, so percentages are indicative, not exact); the top reviews of the 9 core games were read in full; one store screenshot of each
core game was viewed. Developer posts supplied two real sales figures. **None of these games was played by Claude or the owner.** Items are tagged
**[store]**, **[review]**, **[dev]**, **[screenshot]** or **[inference]**. Companion files: [discovery-2026-10-21.md](discovery-2026-10-21.md), [02-zero-stress-king.md](02-zero-stress-king.md),
[03-beam-inc.md](03-beam-inc.md).*

## What they do right

### 1. They sell time, not endless time
Every core game is **3 to 10 hours** at the median positive reviewer (Digseum 2.8, Nodebuster 4.5, Shelldiver 4.8, Bills 5.1, Keep on Mining 5.8, Berry Bury Berry 10.1).
"Short" is stated in the store text or tags of Nodebuster, Digseum, Keep on Mining!, Last Cat, and Bills ("a short active incremental"). About **30% of positive reviews
praise the shortness** ("short and sweet", "valuing your time"; Digseum 38%, Shelldiver 29%, Bills 29%, Nodebuster 30%). The same fact is the top **negative** theme too
(Bills' most-upvoted negative: "just long enough for no refunds", 20 votes; Digseum 38% of its negatives). **[inference]** Short wins when the first hour is excellent and the price is $3 to $6;
it loses when it falls under the price.

### 2. A verb that feels good in the first 30 seconds
Their art and juice carry the first minutes, not the systems. Visible in the screenshots: Bills shows a table of piggy banks exploding in coins with a "Jackpot" pop-up and a stamina bar;
Shelldiver shows a diver shooting jellyfish through bubbles with a labelled depth; Keep on Mining! shows a field of glowing ore piles with laser beams; Nodebuster has a retro CRT look and a glowing
node tree. **[review]** "The artwork, game feel, UI interactions and visual effects are all so on point, so satisfying... makes me want to go another run" (Nodebuster). Polish, art or sound is praised in
about 13% to 35% of positives (Shelldiver 33%, Last Cat 35%, Digseum 34%, Bills 31%). **Compare BEAM Inc.**, which has the same structure but a hairline beam and flat outlines ([03-beam-inc.md](03-beam-inc.md)).

### 3. The same loop, but a session
Eight of the nine core games use **"run, collect, sell or spend, tree, repeat"**: Shelldiver (dive and surface), Nodebuster ("Breach" button, then the node tree), Keep on Mining! (timed session, "End Session"),
Digseum (excavation levels), Last Cat (2-minute runs), You Know The Drill (timed drill runs), Fishing Inc (casts per day, "End Day"), Bills (runs against bills). **BEAM Inc.'s "Start Observation then Open Upgrade Screen" is the same skeleton as
Nodebuster's** (screenshot: "Breach" button beside a node tree with a tooltip showing "+10% attack size, Lv. 4/10"), and Nodebuster is 97% positive with 14,000 reviews.
So the owner's complaint about BEAM is **about execution, not structure**: feel, readable feedback, and the first minutes. **[inference]**

### 4. A pointer verb in most of them
At least **five** have an aim, hover or click verb rather than pure idling: Nodebuster ("you aim at blocks"), Keep on Mining! ("hover your mouse above rocks"; the mining area grows), Shelldiver (a gun in a dive),
Bills (smash), You Know The Drill (steer the drill with the cursor). **[review]** The reviewer who coined "active-incremental" explains it as "gaining resources, updating a skill tree, passively increasing numbers... like increased damage, bigger mining area, more time". That is the
owner's "aim is the only input" idea in a working form. The **warning** is in the negatives: "your cursor/mining area becomes so big you literally don't have to do anything but swirl it around" (Keep on Mining!) and
"this feels like an idle game, just it isn't, as you need to aim your reticle and pick up drops, basically it's boring" (Nodebuster). The active verb must stay worth doing, or be skippable gracefully.

### 5. Personality and a little mystery
Berry Bury Berry is the clearest case. **[screenshot]** A smiling star-headed rainbow guide says "Put a berry in the hole. Pick up the coin. Repeat until bedtime." **[review]** 28% of its positive reviews (and 28% of
negatives) talk about the surprise, lore or horror twist; the top positive (28 votes) is a long story of being surprised at night and "overrun by gnomes". The Last Cat in the Universe is **46% story** in its positives
("made me weep at the end") and is the clearest example of a tiny narrative lifting a $5.59 incremental; its negatives are the incremental half ("no interaction with the cat", "a no from me", 69% of negatives mention the story). Digseum adds a talking museum
character and "a tiny bit of mystery". **[inference]** A single hook line or twist makes players write reviews and tell friends.

### 6. A visible tree with honest tooltips
Nodebuster, Shelldiver and BEAM all show a tree; the winners show **"cost, level, and the stat change"** and let you see what a node needs by hovering (Shelldiver patched that on day 3: "you are now able to see which upgrades
require different collectables by hovering"). The unmet versions are named in negatives: "each upgrade is just some form of x% boost", "the tech tree is incredibly simple with no real choices" (Digseum). **[inference]** Tooltips with before and after values are expected; choices
between differently-shaped nodes are what players want next.

### 7. They respect the player's time and their taste
Shelldiver and Nodebuster both ship accessibility switches: Nodebuster added **disable flashing effects, an alternate colour palette, a CRT toggle, darker screen transitions and "mouse hidden during battle"** (all within three weeks of launch,
three small news posts). Bills added **three save slots, a New Game button that can no longer wipe your save**, a fix for a launch crash, and a coin-flip choice after players called a mechanic unfair. Shelldiver added **a remappable skill-tree key** and hover hints for what each upgrade needs.
**[inference]** Small trust-building fixes, shipped fast, are cheap; compare Zero Stress King's save-wipe and ad complaints.

### 8. Price, discount and launch are deliberate
- Prices are **$2.99 to $6.99**; the best performers per copy sit at **$2.99 to $4.99**. **[dev]** Bills launched at **$6.99 with a 30% launch discount** ("under $5 in the first two weeks") and sold **100,000+ copies in under 72 hours** (developer post, 2026-08-01) after
  **10,000 wishlists in May and 40,000+ a week before launch**, with a free demo from 2026-04-22.
- **[dev]** Shelldiver: "**10,000 copies in the first 24 hours**" and "over 800 reviews" at about three days, after a Next Fest demo in October 2025 and a launch about five weeks later.
- Two growth shapes: **launch spike** (Bills, You Know The Drill: 30% to 33% of all reviews in week 1, about 50% in two weeks) versus **slow-burn word of mouth** (Nodebuster 3% in week 1 and 14% in two weeks, Digseum 7%, Berry Bury Berry 8%). Even the spike games still collect 50 to 150 reviews a day
  three months later (Bills).

### 9. Localisation matters more than it looks
Non-English reviews are **73% of Shelldiver's** (13 languages), 63% of Keep on Mining!'s (11), 52% of Bills' and Rusty's (10 to 11). The English-only games (Nodebuster, Digseum) still have 20% to 23% non-English reviews, but the game with the
widest language list is the biggest seller. **[inference]** Localisation into about 10 languages is worth doing early for this price point; it is cheap relative to the extra audience. (The owner's concept: plan for it.)

### 10. The weak spots are consistent, and avoidable
Keyword matches among negatives (approximate): **grind or "slow"** 31% to 69% (Shelldiver 46%, Digseum 57%, YKTD 69%, Last Cat 57%, Keep on Mining 48%, Nodebuster 42%); **too short** 5% to 38% (Digseum 38%, Last Cat 28%, Bills 22%); **price** up to 45% (YKTD 45%, Bills 41%, Digseum 35%).
Specific, repeated quotes: "the gameplay 10 seconds in and 3 hours later is still the exact same" (Nodebuster), "the game is pretty much the same from start to finish" (Fishing Inc), "visual clutter makes it hard to locate your cursor" (You Know The Drill), "constantly slowed down by unnecessary
animations" (Digseum), "the incremental part is utterly slow... 3-4 two-minute runs to get any upgrades" (Last Cat), "too addictive, could not sleep" (a Shelldiver negative, which is a compliment). **[inference]** Pacing of the first purchase, variety of node types, and readable late-game screens are where the
cheap incrementals lose points.

## The desktop-idler lane (a different game)

Rusty's Retirement (14,924 reviews), Ropuka's Idle Island and Cornerpond are **always-on, bottom-of-the-screen idlers** with median reviewer playtimes of **67 to 153 hours** (mostly left running). They collect many more hours per buyer but are reviewed for
art and "having it in the corner while I work" rather than for active play. Their negatives: "a cute desktop companion, but that is quite literally all it is", "progression is painstakingly slow", and unclear UI. **[inference]** This is a separate product from a single-screen aim incremental; the two can be mixed (Desktop Defender, Everbastion in the discovery file), but the audience and review signals differ.

## Calibrating the "copies per review" rule with real figures

The earlier sales estimate for Zero Stress King used 30x to 50x. These developer-stated numbers now check it:
- **Bills Must Be Paid:** "100k+ copies in less than 72h" (posted 2026-08-01) against **3,811 reviews in the first week** (to 2026-08-04, all languages). Copies by day 7 were at least 100,000, so **about 26x or more, plausibly 30x to 40x** (if day-7 copies were 115k to 150k).
- **Shelldiver:** 10,000 copies in 24 hours; about 800 reviews at about three days (copies by then unknown, plausibly 25,000 to 35,000), **about 30x to 45x**.
So **30x to 40x is a good working figure for a $3 to $7 incremental at launch**; the multiplier probably rises later as discounted and bundled copies add players who do not review. This moves the Zero Stress King central estimate to the **lower half** of the earlier range (60,000 to 80,000). Two data points are a check, not a proof.

## Implications for the owner's concept (inference)

*Concept: a stress-free, single-screen incremental or auto-battler in a space or fantasy theme where the only input is aiming, with an opt-out, and no way to lose.*
1. **The structure is proven.** Round, collect, spend on a tree, repeat is the dominant winning loop; a pointer verb is common. Nothing here argues against it.
2. **The bar is feel.** Write the first-minute checklist from this study: a satisfying hit effect within 5 seconds; a number or pop-up that tells the player they did well; a first upgrade bought within about 30 seconds; no clipped text; a tooltip that shows "A to B, cost".
3. **Keep the first purchase fast.** BEAM's first upgrade took two rounds; Last Cat and YKTD lose points for slow first purchases ("3-4 two-minute runs to get any upgrades").
4. **Give it one hook** (a surprise, a character, a joke) as Berry Bury Berry and Last Cat do. For space or fantasy this can be a tiny story told through upgrade names or a guide character.
5. **Plan for 4 to 8 hours, a clear end, and a reason to stay.** Players punish games that are finished in 2 to 4 hours for $6.99 (Bills) and celebrate games that land at 4 to 6 hours for $3 to $5.
6. **A price near $3 to $5, a 30% launch discount, 8 to 13 languages, a demo and a Next Fest slot** match both sales leaders; use the free demo as the wishlist engine.
7. **Ship the small quality-of-life items from day one**: save slots, accessibility toggles, remappable keys, controller support, a visible stats panel. They are cheap and review-positive.
8. **Make aiming a real choice, not a chore.** The negatives call out "swirl the mouse" and "just click" boredom; make skipping aim cost efficiency (the owner's design) but make aiming reward skill with visible, varied upgrades (width, pierce, split, and so on).
9. **A no-fail design is fine, but needs depth and length** (see Zero Stress King): "no choices" and "same from start to finish" are the cheap incrementals' recurring complaints.

## Per-game notes

- **Shelldiver** ($4.99): a cozy dive-and-sell incremental about a senior turtle selling jellyfish; shoot jellyfish and debris, surface, sell, upgrade a large skill tree. 99% of 18,856. 13 languages, controller support, only three news posts. **Does right:** charm and colour, a satisfying
  collect loop, a clear biome label ("Ocean Shallows, 100m"), fast patch after launch. **Weak spot:** "short numbers-go-up game", 46% of negatives mention slow or grind; one reviewer found it too addictive.
- **Bills Must Be Paid** ($6.99): smash piggy banks to pay bills, prestige and bankruptcy, a "Double or Nothing" coin-flip; 3D, extremely juicy. 12,900 reviews in 12 weeks. **Does right:** a 30% launch discount, 40,000 wishlists, a demo, fast responses to feedback (patch 1.1 within 18 days), save slots. **Weak spot:** "short, nothing special, like the six other incrementals", 4 to 5 hours at $6.99.
- **Nodebuster** ($2.99): experimental incremental, "busting nodes and destroying reality"; you aim a reticle to break nodes in short breaches; retro CRT look; English only. **Does right:** game feel, UI interaction, accessibility toggles; slow-burn word of mouth. **Weak spot:** netcoins gate late progress and force waiting; "upgrades are just x% boosts".
- **Berry Bury Berry** ($5.59 on sale; list $7.99): first-person berries and a hole; surreal, funny, mild horror; demo; controller. **Does right:** a hook in the first minute, a secret layer, personality. **Weak spot:** pace between upgrades; "automation is lame" for hardcore players.
- **Digseum** ($2.99): dig relics, build a museum; minimal pixel art; English only. **Does right:** a clear, tiny loop; a talking museum character; "valuing your time". **Weak spot:** "constantly slowed down by unnecessary animations", no real tree choices.
- **Keep on Mining!** ($2.99): hover to mine, bigger mining area, big tree, timed sessions; "active-incremental". **Does right:** one clear verb that scales. **Weak spot:** gets mindless; some players alleged low-effort/AI-like development (an accusation, unverified).
- **Fishing Inc** ($3.24): casts per day, a fish log; "cozy and repetitive". **Does right:** cozy theme, collection. **Weak spot:** "same from start to finish".
- **The Last Cat in the Universe** ($5.59): space-station AI builds a home for the last cat; short, narrative incremental. **Does right:** an emotional ending. **Weak spot:** the incremental half is slow and the cat is not interactive.
- **You Know The Drill** ($3.74): a drill converting ore into more drill; solo developer; 4 to 5 hours to platinum. **Does right:** a unique feel, sharp tagline. **Weak spot:** late-game visual clutter hides the cursor; slow final stretch.
- **Desktop idlers** (Rusty's Retirement, Ropuka's Idle Island, Cornerpond): see the lane above.

## Method and limits

- Candidates came from the English-review ranking of tag pairs (Incremental with Relaxing, Cute, Cozy, Arcade, Casual) and a judgment filter for short, cheap, paid, high-scoring games ($2.99 to $6.99 plus the desktop lane). **Not included on purpose:**
  Cookie Clicker ($4.99, 94,120 reviews, the genre's origin, not short), Placid Plastic Duck Simulator and Bongo Cat (not incrementals in the same sense), and mid-tier games with fewer than about 1,000 reviews.
- Each review sample is the Steam "helpful" ordering (up to 500 positive, up to 270 negative) and includes duplicate pages; theme percentages are keyword matches on that sample and could move by several points. Quotes are shortened.
- No game was played. The visual notes come from one store screenshot each. Estimated copies and revenue are rules of thumb (PLAYBOOK section 8) except where marked **[dev]**.
- Reproduce: `node tools/steam.mjs summary <id>`, `node tools/steam.mjs news <id>`, and the weekly histogram at `https://store.steampowered.com/appreviewhistogram/<id>`.
