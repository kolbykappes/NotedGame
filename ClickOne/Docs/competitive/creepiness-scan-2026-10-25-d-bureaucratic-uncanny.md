# Creepiness scan D: institutional / bureaucratic uncanny (Stanley Parable, Control, Kentucky Route Zero, Lobotomy Corporation)

Date: 2026-10-25

Method note: cursory scan from public pages only (Wikipedia, critic pieces, Haywire, PCGamesN, Steam threads); nothing was played. Search quality was thin for specific document text; where a detail comes from general knowledge of the game rather than a fetched page it is tagged [inference] and should be verified before relying on it. No sales numbers included.

## 1. The Stanley Parable (2013 original; Ultra Deluxe 2022) - PC/consoles, ~1-2 h per run, many short endings

**What it is.** A narrated first-person walk through an office. You are Employee 427, whose job is pressing buttons as instructed; one day everyone is gone. A British narrator describes what Stanley "does" and reacts when you disobey. [wiki] Ultra Deluxe adds new branches and self-referential material. [wiki]

**The mundane layer.** Room 427, a monitor with button prompts, corridors, a meeting room, posters and slideshows of office cliche. [wiki][critic] The office is deliberately sparse; the developer said this reflects the narrator omitting unimportant everyday objects. [dev]

**The wrong layer.**
- An empty workplace discovered at the start (Stanley is alone in the building). [wiki][critic]
- A dry, formal, increasingly frustrated narrator whose omissions define the space. [wiki]
- Pure rule-bending: a plain instruction ("go through the left door") that you may break, then escalating consequences. [wiki]
- Sound and bare lighting in the stranger rooms (mind control facility, bomb room). [player report: Steam thread]
- Late-game reveal that the narration is finite: a wall listing every line the narrator has said (Confusion ending). [wiki]

**Most-cited unsettling moment.** The Broom Closet ending: standing in the closet while the narrator runs out of patience, then declares you dead. [critic] Also the baby-and-fire game. [critic]

**Borrowable for Noted.**
- Make absence a typed, plain sentence on a form ("Occupancy today: 0 / Expected: 0 / Variance: none noted") rather than shown. [inference]
- A job instruction that can be disobeyed once with a quiet, procedural consequence (a log line, a deducted coin), never a jump scare. [inference]
- Closet-style dead end: one room where lingering produces slowly escalating terminal text. [inference]

**Avoid.** The comedy/meta-narrator voice; Noted must keep a straight face. Do not make a voiced narrator.

## 2. Control (2019) - PC/consoles, ~12-15 h [inference]

**What it is.** Third-person action game in the Oldest House, HQ of the Federal Bureau of Control, a Brutalist building that is larger inside than out and rearranges itself. [wiki] Documents, audio logs, TV broadcasts and live-action training videos carry the lore. [wiki]

**The mundane layer.** A 1970s-style government office: staplers, desk lamps, generic watercolors, binders, pneumatic capsules; resembles a passport office or DMV. [critic: PCGamesN] Compartmentalized mid-century office design. [wiki]

**The wrong layer.**
- Memos in dry bureaucratic voice about strange things: a banned-items list that puts number 2 pencils, rubber ducks and ketchup bottles beside weapons; a note about an executive restroom missing for weeks. [critic]
- Upbeat corporate cheer applied to paranormal hazards (slogan-style reminders about suspicion). [critic]
- Redactions used for comedy and dread. [critic]
- Ordinary objects (fridge, floppy disc, carousel horse) classed as dangerous "Altered Items," never fully understood by the Bureau. [wiki]
- Rooms lit red and warped into distorted copies of normal offices, restored to normal when cleared. [critic]
- Training videos made stilted and amateur on purpose. [wiki]

**Most-cited moment.** The Ashtray Maze and the Oldest House itself as a character; among memos, the missing-restroom style of note is the one critics quote. [critic][wiki]

**Borrowable for Noted.**
- Banned-items poster in the break room that lists mundane things next to one wrong one (e.g. "reflective mugs"). [inference]
- Missing-room memo: a notice about a room that "has not been located since the last audit" that the player cannot find on the map. [inference]
- Altered-Item inventory tags: a stapler or mug with a catalog sticker and a hazard number, nothing explained. [inference]
- One red-lit variant of a known room, same layout, shown once. [inference]

**Avoid.** Combat, Hiss-style enemies, big set-piece architecture, FMV (cost).

## 3. Kentucky Route Zero (2013-2020, five acts) - PC/consoles/mobile, ~6-8 h [inference]

**What it is.** Point-and-click interactive fiction by Cardboard Computer about a delivery driver, Conway, searching for an address on a hidden road in Kentucky. [wiki] Choices shape characterization more than plot. [wiki]

**The mundane layer.** A gas station (Equus Oils), a delivery job, forms, a municipal-style office, an old whiskey distillery. [wiki]

**The wrong layer.**
- The Bureau of Reclaimed Spaces: you need an "ingestion notice" from inside before you may even get directions, followed by a chain of misdirections to reach the senior clerk. [wiki]
- Office inside a cathedral; no doorway marks inside from outside; unfinished edges open to weather; a recorded sermon playing to an evicted, absent congregation; organ left on the mezzanine. [critic: Haywire]
- A floor of the elevator for bears; they do nothing. [critic]
- Forms that approve repurposing buildings (hospital to car dealership, basketball court to kennel, distillery to graveyard); the last item is the quiet wrong note. [critic: Haywire]
- Act II is initially light and funny, which sets up the sadness later. [critic]

**Most-cited moment.** The Bureau scene (cathedral office, bears, the graveyard-conversion form) and the ghost/elegy finale. [critic]

**Borrowable for Noted.**
- A "conversion approvals" clipboard where mundane repurposings are listed and one item is wrong ("Storage Room 3 to: Quiet Room"). [inference]
- Prerequisite loops: a form needing a slip that is issued only by the room you cannot enter yet. [inference]
- An office whose edges are unfinished (an unlit, unwalled stretch behind a door) without any explanation. [inference]
- A recorded announcement looping into empty rooms. [inference]

**Avoid.** Heavy literary poetry and large cast; it relies on voices and people.

## 4. Lobotomy Corporation (2018) - PC, dozens of hours, Unity, Project Moon (Korea)

**What it is.** Roguelike management sim: run a facility that extracts energy from "Abnormalities" using employees, over repeated days. [wiki][store] Premise draws on SCP and Warehouse 13. [wiki]

**The mundane layer.** Departments, daily quotas, employee rosters and stat sheets, work orders, management guidelines per entity. [wiki][inference]

**The wrong layer.**
- A cold case file per Abnormality: restrained little fable of origin, no cutscenes. [critic: review summary]
- Safe-handling rules stated plainly as work instructions, each hiding a trap you learn by employee deaths. [inference]
- Human life treated as an energy line item. [critic]
- Cheerful names for horrifying entities; a "Today's Shy Look" entry whose origin is a person carving smiling faces into their skin. [wiki: fan wiki]
- Gentle entities that turn cruel after several days. [inference]
- The 402-blocked fan wiki page could not be fetched for exact text; treat phrasing as unverified.

**Most-cited moment.** Not reliably sourced in this scan. Players commonly cite Nothing There, which mimics human speech by wearing victims' skin. [player report: low reliability]

**Borrowable for Noted.**
- Per-object "Handling Notes" cards (3 lines) attached to props: dry origin sentence, two procedural rules, one oddly specific warning. [inference]
- A rule that is only revealed useful after it matters (e.g. "Do not log readings between 02:10 and 02:14"). [inference]
- Days that reuse the same task with one handling note changed. [inference]

**Avoid.** Real-time breach/management complexity; the heavy dialogue and rough translation were criticized. [critic: RPS via wiki]

## Patterns across these four

- **Plain statement, impossible content.** Stanley's directions, Control's banned-items list, Lobotomy Corp's handling rules and KRZ's forms all state the wrong thing in the same flat voice as the right thing. The pattern is one wrong item among ordinary ones, never labelled as odd.
- **Tone recipe for deadpan corporate voice.** Use passive voice, reference numbers and routine request verbs ("please find out where it went"), and let the writer show no alarm at the event. Add a small cheerful corporate slogan on top. Never add an adjective that tells the reader to be scared; keep the sentence ordinary and let the object of the sentence be impossible. [critic][inference]
- **Space does the work too.** Office architecture is lightly wrong (cathedral office, unfinished edges, a missing restroom, empty rooms) without monsters. [critic]
- **Procedures that loop.** KRZ's prerequisite chains and Lobotomy Corp's handling rules both use paperwork as the trap; cheap in text, strong in feel. [wiki][inference]
- **Ordinary objects get catalog labels** (Altered Items, Abnormalities) and are not explained. [wiki]
- **Absence beats presence.** Stanley's empty office and KRZ's evicted congregation echo Noted's constraint: evidence of people, never people.
