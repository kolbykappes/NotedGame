## Open design problem: keeping the aim input interesting

Owner, 2026-10-21 (from Zero Stress King): the beam "doesn't seem to matter at all, except chasing down stragglers... otherwise it's a novelty for the first 5m and then you just don't care." Evidence: kill counters show the monument at 3 of 5,591 kills late in the run, versus 405 and 533 in the first boss waves ([02-zero-stress-king.md](02-zero-stress-king.md), batches 2 and 3). Next research step: find games where the pointer stays relevant deep into the run, and what makes it so (ShredDead's aimed ring, timed or targeted abilities, stragglers that matter, bosses that need the player).

## Open design problems from Zero Stress King (owner's hands-on, 2026-10-21)

1. **Late upgrades do not feel meaningful.** "None of them feel super meaningful"; "VERY little feedback" about what a purchase did; unlock nodes feel good, percentage nodes do not. Terms such as "loot drop" are never defined. See [02-zero-stress-king.md](02-zero-stress-king.md).
2. **The opt-out for aiming is weak.** The paid "All monuments automation" sweeps slowly in an arc and rarely hits, so it does not replace the player.
3. **Boss as diagnostic is the idea to keep.** A no-lose boss that tests speed, fire rate and damage type is the strongest idea in the game (owner), but the game does not tell you what you lacked.
4. **No-lose and impact pull against each other.** With a lava moat and an endless stream, nothing you buy visibly changes the outcome; the player raises enemy health and spawns on purpose for income, which cancels the felt gain. 50+ affordable upgrades and no "I am short on X" signal (owner: "I just don't get how to make the game feel more impactful"). **This is the main design question for ClickOne's no-lose structure.**
5. **Too many units.** Owner: "whether I have 63 archers or 78 archers - who cares. It's just more noise on screen. I'd reduce the entire quantity of units by an order of magnitude." Candidate rule for ClickOne: few, individually distinct units.

## Cross-game findings

One game only so far, so these are single-source and provisional.
- **A no-fail incremental splits players.** ShredDead's demo threads are about 8 against and 5 for the lack of a lose condition (manual count, 2026-10-20). Whatever the stress-free positioning is, it needs a tension source.
- **No-lose incrementals exist and one sold.** Zero Stress King (Mar 2026, $6.99, 88% of 2,008 reviews) markets "no losing". About 4 of its 117 negatives mention missing challenge, while about 29 say it is too short. Increvaders ("no game overs"), Farming with lasers ("no death, progress only") and Everbastion follow. Length, depth and ads were the problems, not the missing fail state (2026-10-21, keyword counts approximate).
- **The "buy harder enemies" economy appears in at least two games** (ShredDead, Zero Stress King), and a 100-hour Zero Stress King player asks for more of it ("more risk = more reward").
- **2,000 reviews is top-3% territory.** About 3% of 2025 releases reached 1,000 reviews (calculator-site data). Zero Stress King has about 3% of buyers reviewing if it sold about 60,000 copies; treat all sales figures as 2x-wide estimates (PLAYBOOK section 8).
- **The first five minutes decide it (owner hands-on, 2026-10-21).** BEAM Inc.'s first rounds are 10 shots and 2 kills for 4 currency; the owner dropped it after 30 minutes ("awful in terms of mechanics and looks"). A demo reviewer also said it was cleared in about 30 to 40 minutes and not worth buying "unless the price is near zero". Compare Zero Stress King, which reviewers praise for polish and instant feedback.
- **Players expect feedback tools** in this genre (damage and income stats, drop clarity, pausing menus), and ask for them within days of a demo.

- **The best short incrementals use the same skeleton as BEAM Inc. and Zero Stress King (run, collect, spend on a tree) and win on feel, charm and a hook.** Nodebuster's round-plus-node-tree loop is near-identical to BEAM Inc.'s and has 14,119 reviews at 97%. Details: [short-incrementals-what-works.md](short-incrementals-what-works.md).
- **Real launch figures (developer posts):** Shelldiver 10,000 copies in 24 hours; Bills Must Be Paid 100,000+ copies in under 72 hours. They back a 30x to 40x copies-per-review figure for $3 to $7 incrementals at launch.

## Market read

Focused short incrementals cluster at $1 to $7 and 4 to 8 hours with Very Positive scores (86% to 97%). *Sir, We Have an Orc Problem* ($9.99, 4,707 reviews, 87%) shows the top end works. Details and caveats: [discovery-2026-10-21.md](discovery-2026-10-21.md). **Updated 2026-10-21:** the top short incrementals at $3 to $7 hold roughly 250,000 to 750,000 estimated copies each (rule of thumb), 5 to 10 times the defence-style peers; see the topic study. **Gap found 2026-10-21:** the top-reviewed short incrementals ($3 to $6, 97% to 99% positive) have 8,000 to 19,000 reviews, far above the defence-style peers (details in the discovery file's addendum).
