## What it is
- A **free idle tower-building simulator**: you raise the world's tallest tower floor by floor by mining stone, making bricks and moving them up with a crane. [store]
- **Origin and platforms:** the itch page credits **GeneralVimes** as the original author and says the game was "played by ten million people in web browsers" [dev claim]; Airapport publishes the **Windows, Linux and macOS downloads, Android and iOS** versions. Launch post 2020-08-15. Not on Steam (searched: no match), and no Reddit threads found.
- **Business model:** **free with optional rewarded ads** and, on iOS, **in-app purchases from $0.99 to $15.99** (ad skips, golden tools, rubies). [store] It is a mobile-first, free-to-play design, not a premium game.
- **Ratings:** 4.6 of 5 from 113 on itch; 4.8 of 5 from 416 on the App Store (listed as "Idle Tower Builder: Miner City", v1.9.7). [store]
- **Longevity:** six years of updates: 1.0.9 (2020), winter graphics (2020), **modding enabled (2021)**, 1.4.5 and 1.5.0 (2023), 1.7 with **characters, TODO tasks and an HTML5 version (2025-02)**, 1.8.0 **achievements (2025-09, "the thing players asked the most")**, spring graphics (2026-03), and a **new Eiffel Tower to build on 2026-11-11**. Weekly **bonus codes** are posted on Discord, Reddit and YouTube. [dev]

## How it plays (from the store text)
| System | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Production chain** | Quarry stone, process it into bricks. Each floor needs many more bricks than the last. |
| **Crane** | Made from planks (lumbermill); lifts bricks to higher floors. |
| **Forest** | **Wood is depletable**; watering (a well, or a rain-casting thundercloud) restores it faster. |
| **Workers** | Wheelbarrow workers carry things between sites; a drummer speeds them up; later an **elephant** carries stone but needs food. |
| **Food** | Grown on a field, stored in a haystack. |
| **Money** | Sell resources; **tourists arrive by zeppelin after floor 5**. |
| **Automation** | A **caravanserai** raises prices; a **camel** moves any resource automatically. An **auto-clicker** means little manual clicking. |
| **Technologies** | Unlocked as the tower grows: e.g. a **clock that speeds up time**, a **helicopter** that boosts stone. |
| **Space and science** | Past a height, space unlocks **science** (dropped apples, telescope observations of stars and comets), spent on **permanent lab upgrades** in the basement. |
| **Prestige** | Restarting earns **Golden Bricks** that boost tap power, facility output and market prices. |

**Controls and length:** not stated on the page. Tapping is implied ("tap power"). One reviewer calls it "a good 10 to 15 minute daily game". [player report]

## Reception [player report]
- Praise: **no timer**, nice upgrades, **optional ads worth watching for the bonuses**, relaxing, "casual time-waster".
- Complaints: **"repetitive and boring"** after a while, wants **more uses for Golden Bricks** (an ascension system or a skill tree), and a bug-like note that the camel does not multiply sales for some resources.
- The itch threads are about **macOS and browser-mode** support, not design.

## What is useful for ClickOne [inference]
1. **A living, readable world.** Every activity is a visible character or building (workers with wheelbarrows, a drummer, a zeppelin of tourists, a camel hauling goods). **Cause and effect can be watched.** This is the opposite of Zero Stress King's counters, and it echoes the finding that the impactful games make **progress visible in the scene** (see [impact-study-2026-10-21.md](impact-study-2026-10-21.md)).
2. **Automation as an in-world unit.** The camel, the elephant and the drummer **are** the automation, each with its own need (food, water), so unlocking automation is a **story beat and a new thing to look at**, not a menu toggle. Zero Stress King's "Automation" is a node.
3. **Pressure without losing:** a **depletable forest** gives a gentle constraint you manage (water it) rather than a fail state.
4. **Seasons and new towers** (winter, spring, a coming Eiffel Tower) kept a six-year-old game alive without new mechanics. A **cheap, content-light way to extend life**; not relevant to a 4 to 5 hour game.
5. **Not a fit for the owner's targeting idea.** Input is tapping and an auto-clicker; there is no aimed verb. And the **model (free, ads, IAP)** is the opposite of a $5 premium game, so do not read its ratings against Steam titles.
