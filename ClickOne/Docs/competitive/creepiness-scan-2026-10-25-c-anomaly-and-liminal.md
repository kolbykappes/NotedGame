# Creepiness scan C: anomaly-spotting and liminal interfaces (2026-10-25)

Cursory public-data scan. Nothing was played or downloaded. Some pages returned 403/429 (Slant, TV Tropes, Prima, one Steam guide), so gaps are flagged. No sales figures included.

## 1. The Exit 8 (2023)

**What it is.** First-person walking game in a looping Japanese subway corridor, Kotake Create, PC/consoles/mobile, VR version later. About 15-30 minutes per clean run [wiki].

**The mundane layer.** Walk a plain tiled passage past a sign, ads, three doors and a salaryman. The only verb is "does anything look different?" [wiki].

**The wrong layer.**
- Rule is one sentence: no anomaly means keep walking, an anomaly means turn back; eight correct calls in a row, any mistake resets to Exit 0 [wiki]. The baseline is taught by repetition alone.
- Anomalies span a range [store/wiki, guide]. Subtle: posters slowly growing, a crooked light, a doorknob mid-door, a face in the floor tiles, altered sign text. Obvious: red water filling the corridor, black fluid from a vent, an upside-down sign, a missing door. Dangerous: figures that reach you if you do not retreat, e.g. two suited men mid-corridor, a camouflaged figure at the far end, the briefcase man sprinting [guide].
- The recurring human, the briefcase man, is itself an anomaly carrier (taller, smiling, face see-through, turning to look) [guide].
- No fixed order or ramp inside a run, so the escalation is the player's growing paranoia plus the reset penalty [critic]. The rare anomaly appears only on an unforgiving streak.
- Developer built it in about nine months, partly from real stations; the endless loop is two identical passages joined [wiki].

**Most-cited moment.** Red water flooding the corridor, and the figure that is only noticed on a second look [critic/player report, inference on ranking].

**Borrowable for Noted.**
- One baseline room that the player repeats, with the day's "wrongness" as a delta against a memorised layout (a sign, a poster, a count of doors).
- Subtle anomalies that slowly change over the day clock (posters "growing" idea): cheap, a scale lerp.
- A single recurring prop or object that is wrong a little more each visit.
- Two-tier: most anomalies harmless and checkable, a rare one that punishes looking away.

**Avoid.** Cheap jump scares drew critic complaints [critic]; reset-on-mistake punishment does not fit a 60-second job loop.

## 2. I'm on Observation Duty, series (first game 2018, Notovia)

**What it is.** Spot-the-difference surveillance horror: watch camera feeds of a room and report anomalies. PC (Unity) plus later mobile; about half an hour a session [store: itch]. Series now eight-plus entries.

**The mundane layer.** Stare at a bank of CCTV feeds through a night shift (series: 00:00 to 06:00), then file a typed report with anomaly type and location [store].

**The wrong layer.**
- Anomaly families (seen in later-entry guides, so partly [inference] for the first game): object moved, object vanished, extra object, door opens, light changes, picture changes, intruder, distortion, camera or screen malfunction, "abyss" hole [player report/guide].
- Subtle changes are paired with outright intruders, so the player never knows which register comes next. Some anomalies persist until reported (a missing camera stays gone) [guide].
- Escalation is time-based: anomalies spawn at intervals, with a grace period early on; a cap of three active at once, and a fourth ends the run in a red screen [player report, entry 3 guide].
- Report UI forces you to name the wrongness, which makes noticing an act, not a jump scare.
- Scale: a single market map in entry 5 catalogues about 50 anomalies in 12 categories across six areas [player guide]. Secrets and a hidden ending sit beyond the main shift (a code entered after an input-type anomaly in game one) [player report].

**Most-cited moment.** The intruder who is simply standing in a feed, or a figure that moves closer between glances; not verified for game one specifically.

**Borrowable for Noted.**
- A "report the wrongness" step at the complaint terminal: preset anomaly types the player files.
- Anomaly families as a tiny data table (moved, vanished, extra, changed text) reused across every room.
- A grace period on day one, then one new family per day.
- Unreported anomalies persist, so nights accumulate.

**Avoid.** Growing-hole-type "abyss" anomalies and any human intruder; both break constraints.

## 3. Please, Don't Touch Anything (2015, Four Quarters)

**What it is.** One screen, one console with a red button, pixel art, short (reviewers around two hours); later 3D VR and console ports [wiki/store]. About 25 endings [store/player report].

**The mundane layer.** Press, wait, turn, enter codes on a control panel while "covering for a coworker."

**The wrong layer.**
- Escalation is mechanical: endings 1-3 are waiting, twenty presses, a hidden switch; later endings reuse one code to open a lever, a ten-digit keypad, hidden panels, tumblers and a final recess [player report].
- Tone swings between silly and ominous: "Bloody Face," "Illuminati?" (flash of symbols, then a fake crash that disables the restart lever), a gooey creature from the console, an ending with an eye pictogram you are told not to press [player report].
- The machine changes under the player: panels break open, the reset is removed. Wrongness is that the safe interface mutates.
- Restart lights and posters track progress, so the room itself records your history [player report].

**Most-cited moment.** The fake-crash ending that removes the restart lever [player report; moderate confidence]. No strong critic essay on its horror found.

**Borrowable for Noted.**
- A console the player has already learned that gains one extra, forbidden control per day.
- A fake-crash beat once, late.
- A wall display that quietly tallies what the player did, for the organization.

**Avoid.** Meme humour and pixel-gag endings; they dilute dread.

## 4. Hypnospace Outlaw (2019, Tendershoot / No More Robots)

**What it is.** Win9x-style desktop simulating a 1999 internet visited in sleep; you are a volunteer moderator. PC, later consoles; no official length found [wiki].

**The mundane layer.** Open a case, browse linked pages, find the violation, flag it, earn HypnoCoin, buy programs and pets [wiki].

**The wrong layer.**
- Baseline is established by pages that are silly and kitschy, tone deadpan and sincere [critic: Game Informer].
- Early cases are simple; later ones need new software and lead off-site [critic]. Story arrives in fragments hidden in the clutter.
- The dark turn is plot, not jumpscare: a buggy alpha build bricks your headband, a copyright case leads to the executive's own page, you are removed from duty, then a later update kills users and the service is shut down [wiki].
- Creepiness lives in interface texture: faintly eerie 3D renders, cursor trails, and the premise that you reach it while asleep [critic].
- Hidden pages and downloads reward off-path browsing [critic].

**Most-cited moment.** The network update that crashes headbands and causes deaths, reframing all the earlier jokes [wiki; critic ranking not found].

**Borrowable for Noted.**
- Humor-then-dread: let early memos read as dumb corporate jokes, then reuse the same template for a darker one.
- Optional hidden pages or items in the terminal for obsessive players.
- An in-world software update that quietly changes the job's tools.

**Avoid.** Dense lore pages; Noted has no other people and a small content budget.

## Patterns across these four

- The baseline is taught by repetition of a plain, ugly, boring space; deviation reads only because the player has memorised normal.
- Two registers coexist: small, deniable wrongness and rare unmistakable events. Both are needed.
- Escalation comes from the system, not new assets: time pressure, caps on active anomalies, a console that mutates, a reset penalty.
- Wrongness is cheap when it is a delta of an existing asset (scale, text, count, light colour, position) rather than new models.
- The human element is implied or minimal (a recurring figure, a page author) and carries more dread when it recurs slightly altered.
- Figure for a small game: The Exit 8 reportedly has about 30 anomalies in total [critic: a single guide, treat as approximate]; one Observation Duty map alone lists about 50 across 12 categories [player guide]. Inference for Noted: roughly 20-30 distinct oddities grouped into 8-10 families, each family varied across rooms, is enough to feel solid.
