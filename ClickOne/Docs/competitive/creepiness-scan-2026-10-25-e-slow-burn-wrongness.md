# Creepiness scan E: slow-burn wrongness in cozy/mundane loops (Dredge, Not For Broadcast, DDLC Plus, Inscryption)

Date: 2026-10-25. Summary scan from public pages only (store, wiki, critic, dev article, player threads). Nothing played. Tags: [store] [critic] [dev] [player report] [wiki] [inference]. Source depth varies: Dredge and DDLC are well covered, Not For Broadcast (NFB) is thin on horror because it is not really a horror game.

---

## 1. Dredge (2023, PC/console, Black Salt Games, roughly 8-12h [inference])

**What it is.** Fishing-trading game in a small archipelago; a cozy day loop of fish, sell, upgrade the boat, take errands, with a night loop that turns hostile. [store][wiki]

**Mundane layer.** Sail, catch fish in minigames, sell, buy hull and rod upgrades, run town errands. Daytime is deliberately peaceful and disarming; the team wanted it relaxing enough that you stay out past the point you meant to return. [dev]

**Wrong layer.**
- The first sign is the aberrations: fish in patches of disturbed water with a green glowing particle, which are worth more money. Over half the fish list is aberrant, so the wrongness is also the reward. [wiki]
- Night is rule-based: darkness raises a panic meter (an eye icon that flickers more red), light, daytime and resting in town lower it. At high panic you get screen distortion, hostile hazards, and mirages (fake islands, houses, wrecks, boats) that do not exist. [wiki]
- Townspeople, including the Mayor, nag you to stop going out and rest. [wiki]
- Fog shader centered on the player hides large things; the devs kept monsters unseen as long as possible because reveals weaken them. [dev]
- First shiver: the first night out, when rocks appear that were not there in daylight. [player report]

**Most-cited moment.** Panicking at night, with a narrowed field of view, apparitions and sound closing in, and a "safe dock" that is a mirage. [critic][player report]

**Borrowable for Noted.**
- Daytime-safe / "late" rule: the longer you stay past the 60s day, the more the room changes (a light dies, a door log entry appears). Cheap timer.
- Mirage: a task item or door that exists only while a "stress" value is high and vanishes when you look at it twice.
- Wrongness as reward: the oddest drive or mail pays more coins.
- Nagging NPC voice via terminal presets telling you to go home.

**Avoid.** Jump-scare leviathans; one reviewer disliked them as the only weak part. [player report]

## 2. Not For Broadcast (2020 early access, 1.0 January 2022, PC; consoles 2023; roughly 10h [critic])

**What it is.** FMV control-room sim: you direct a nightly news broadcast for a creeping authoritarian government, cutting cameras on a two-second delay. [wiki][store]

**Mundane layer.** Pick headlines and ads, cut cameras, bleep swears, fix signal, then a visual-novel interlude about your family flat; grade affects pay. [wiki]

**Wrong layer.** Not horror but drift: a comic satire turns bleak as the regime censors, nationalizes and goes to war, ending in nuclear strikes; the news show is later repackaged in softer formats. [wiki][inference: the job never changes, the content does]
- The routine stays identical while what passes through your hands darkens. The first sense of wrongness is a small request to do something slightly dishonest on air. [inference]
- Your pay is tied to compliance, so complicity is a mechanical choice, not a cutscene. [wiki]
- Incident choices carry back into the control room. [wiki]
- Critics describe it as funny and disquieting, "squirming in your seat." [critic]

**Most-cited moment.** I found no consensus; I did not find a single scene cited. Likely candidates are the regime events inside broadcast footage. [inference]

**Borrowable for Noted.**
- Keep the task identical and change only the content: same "sort mail" loop, but memos and labels drift (a department that did not exist on day one).
- A tiny feed of "approved notices" at the night shop that slowly change tone.
- Pay tied to a small compliance choice (log the thermostat as told, or as it is).
- Previous-day review screen showing what you did not use.

**Avoid.** Heavy-handed commentary; Polygon-style "laying it on thick" was the early criticism. [critic]

## 3. Doki Doki Literature Club (Plus) (2017; Plus 2021, PC/console; roughly 4h core [critic])

**What it is.** Free-to-start visual novel, a cute high-school poetry club that turns into a fourth-wall horror game. [wiki]

**Mundane layer.** Write poems by picking words, choose which girl to spend time with, read cheerful club scenes, nothing happens for a long time. [wiki]

**Wrong layer.**
- The clean stretch is long: roughly the first 1.5 to 2 hours of reading before the first real rupture (a club member's suicide). [critic][player report]
- Before that there are tells for the attentive: poem word choices, hidden poems revealing abuse and self-harm. [wiki]
- After the rupture the game restarts with the member removed and others not remembering her; glitched text and distorted sprites increase. [wiki]
- Persistence is the big trick: deleted characters stay gone across runs, and the game demands you edit its files to advance. [wiki]
- The standard ending deletes the game during credits. [wiki]

**Most-cited moment.** Yuri's scene (the broken script leaving you with her body), the poem error screen with Monika appearing, and private scenes with a bleeding eye. [wiki][player report] One player reported their PC turning on at 4am, playing the theme; unverified. [player report]

**Borrowable for Noted.**
- A long, genuinely clean first two days; make the first anomaly small and then undeniable.
- Persistence across days: a coffee machine, drive or door that stays "gone" after you lose it; the world remembers what you broke.
- Plain-text text glitch on the terminal preset list (a preset becomes illegible).
- A save-state beat that mentions a name nobody else remembers.

**Avoid.** The pacing complaint: terror takes too long to arrive for some. [critic] Also avoid real-world file tampering, which is out of scope for a small project and has trust costs.

## 4. Inscryption (2021, PC then consoles; roughly 10-12h [inference])

**What it is.** Roguelike deck-builder in three acts, framed as a haunted game disk; Act 1 is a first-person card duel against a dealer in a cabin. [wiki][store]

**Mundane layer.** Draw cards, pay blood or bones, beat the boss, repeat; between fights you walk around a cabin and solve small object puzzles. [wiki][critic]

**Wrong layer.**
- Wrong from minute one: a looming figure across a dark table. The dread is atmosphere, not jump scares. [critic][player report]
- Rule weirdness is the engine: cards talk and beg not to be sacrificed, you pay with your own organs, losing too often turns you into a death card that can reappear. [critic][wiki]
- A fight where the screen flickers and the dealer's eyes turn red marks a rule change mid-match. [critic]
- Frame breaks: the game is revealed as found footage of a content creator, and it uses the real computer's details. [wiki]

**Most-cited moment.** The cabin dealer's eyes and the red flicker; later acts are praised less for mood. [critic]

**Borrowable for Noted.**
- One fixed, eerie room where the same loop plays; shift only one rule per day.
- Death card analogue: a log entry or tag using the previous you.
- Eyes in the dark: a vent or monitor that is lit one day, dark the next.
- Items that talk through prop text (labels that say please).

**Avoid.** Changing genre midway; reviewers felt Acts 2-3 lost the mood. [critic]

## Patterns across these four

- **The routine stays mechanically stable and wrongness rides on top.** Fishing, newscasting, poem-writing and card duels never change their verbs; the content or consequence mutates. For Noted, keep all jobs the same shape across the 6 hours and only change what they touch.
- **Wrongness that pays.** Dredge aberrations earn more, NFB complicity earns pay, Inscryption sacrifices improve the deck. The player cooperates with the creepiness, which is stronger than watching it. Tie weird items to higher coin value.
- **Persistence converts a scare into dread.** DDLC's deleted characters, Inscryption's death cards, Dredge's panic carry forward. Something broken on day 4 should still be broken on day 9.
- **Pacing: clean fraction before first wrongness varies by what the wrongness is.** DDLC is about 1.5-2h of a roughly 4h core (40-50% [inference]); Dredge shows its first aberration inside the first hour of an ~8-12h run (under 10% [inference]); Inscryption and NFB are off or drifting from near the start. For Noted a safe target is roughly 10-15% clean (day 1-2 of the 5-6h run), then a first plainly-off thing, then a deniable escalation.
- **Authority nags, rules are legible.** Dredge's Mayor, panic meter and light rules make the wrongness a system the player can read and fear. Give Noted a visible stress/late-shift rule rather than random scares.
- **Genre switches cost mood.** Both Inscryption (Acts 2-3) and arguably DDLC (after the reveal) are criticised or debated after changing form; keep Noted in one room-set and one loop.
