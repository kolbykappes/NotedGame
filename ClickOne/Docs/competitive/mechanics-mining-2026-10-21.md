# Ten games to mine for Rack Farm mechanics

*2026-10-21. Roguelites, action games and lane or wave defense, chosen against the pieces of [rack-farm-v1.md](../concept/rack-farm-v1.md): creatures walking at plug bays, a first-person weapon set swapped across system types, room-level tricks, recycling for upgrades, a weekly structure with a quota-style meter, and a short, simple, bright feel. Sources: Steam store text and the five most-helpful positive and four most-helpful negative English reviews per game. Nothing played. Evidence tags: **[store]**, **[player report]**, **[inference]**. For ULTRAKILL and DOOM Eternal the sampled reviews were mostly jokes and account complaints, so what I say about their combat design is **[inference] from the store text and general knowledge**, not from the reviews.*

## What each one offers, and the warning that comes with it

**1. Orcs Must Die! 3** (traps and weapons against hordes; third person)
- **Mine:** traps as the "room verbs" next to your own weapons, a loadout of both, and a heavy emphasis on funny, readable enemy types.
- **Warning** [player report]: many players say **the traps feel "weak and expensive"** and the game **drifted toward direct shooting** ("less an action tower defense game with some 3PV action, more of a f..." in a review cut off in my sample; another says it moves "further into FPS"); one solo player says "traps do most of the work" once learned. Patch 1.2 rebalanced traps.
- **For Rack Farm:** if your weapons can solve plugged-in creatures, **nobody will use the room tricks**. Make plugged-in creatures **mostly immune to weapons** so the water, breaker and valve are the answer.

**2. Sanctum 2** (first-person shooter plus tower defense)
- **Mine:** a first-person game where you also manage lanes; **passive reload "makes weapon switching and using all your guns... a necessity"** [player report]. That is your swap mechanic, solved by one rule.
- **Warning** [player report]: "the right combination of character and weapon can do more damage than all your towers combined", and the guns lack impact.
- **For Rack Farm:** the same lesson as Orcs Must Die!. Also, **make weapons feel like they hit.**

**3. Plants vs. Zombies** (lane defense; 2D)
- **Mine:** each enemy type is readable and **demands a specific answer** (the original's pole-vaulters, balloons and diggers), and **every level adds something new**. Level variations (night, fog, pool) change the board without new systems [store, inference]. A stress-free side mode ("Zen Garden") sits next to the campaign [store].
- **Warning:** none about design; complaints are technical (resolution, support). The best-reviewed game on the list at $4.99.
- **For Rack Farm:** the template for **one new creature, one new answer, and a weekly rhythm**. Ship an in-game "almanac" so each creature's counter is readable.

**4. Dome Keeper** (dig for resources, then defend; roguelite)
- **Mine:** a **day phase of resource gathering, then a wave**; deliver what you carry to the base to buy upgrades; "do you risk delving deeper or return?" It is $4.49 with 22.7K reviews [store].
- **Warning** [player report]: "you just keep doing the same thing", "too little content", "the runs don't really change", and one reviewer "rolling my eyes when the little timer went off and I needed to return to the surface". A mid-game update brought "no in-game explanation of changes".
- **For Rack Farm:** avoid a **forced trip home on a timer**; make each week change something visible; explain updates in-game.

**5. ULTRAKILL** (arena FPS) and **6. DOOM Eternal** (FPS) [inference]
- **Mine:** each weapon has a **distinct role** and the player **swaps constantly** because different enemies call for different tools; the player is rewarded for fast, varied use, and information (a flash, a color, a sound) tells you which tool to reach for. The store text for DOOM Eternal lists "take what you need from your enemies" (kills give health, armor and ammo).
- **Warning:** these are fast, hard, high-skill games, which is **not** the owner's "simple and bright". Mine the idea of **role clarity**, not the speed.
- **For Rack Farm:** five weapons with five unmistakable roles, each paired with a **visible cue on the system it works on** (a color or a sound).

**7. Slime Rancher** (cute first-person ranching with a vacuum pack)
- **Mine:** the **vacuum as a core verb** in a bright, cute, first-person world. Can 'Em All commenters compared that game to it.
- **Warning** [player report]: **"unrewarding tedium"** from an inventory so small you run back and forth; and a reviewer who felt "existential emptiness" about piling cute slimes into "sad little forcefield boxes to exploit for big cash money".
- **For Rack Farm:** (a) the vacuum is workable but **give it generous capacity**; (b) players **do feel guilt** about cute things, which supports making the creatures **cute and scheming, so the joke and the license to smash both land**.

**8. Roboquest** (first-person roguelite shooter)
- **Mine:** a first-person roguelite with persistent upgrades, bright colors and satisfying gunplay.
- **Warning** [player report]: the roguelite layer is the weakness: **"the upgrades are miniscule"**, **"a good game gated behind dozens of hours of a much worse game"** because the upgrade pools are full of weak options until you grind meta-currency, and **"lack of payoff, lack of the power fantasy"**.
- **For Rack Farm:** every upgrade you sell with recycled currency must be **felt on the next day**. That is your owner's Zero Stress King lesson again.

**9. MULLET MADJACK** (short arcade FPS)
- **Mine:** **health is a clock and kills refill it**, short rooms, and "90% arcade fun, 10% roguelite". One reviewer says the 10-second clock felt punishing until an **assist option** appeared.
- **Warning** [player report]: about **three hours**, **"shallow and repetitive"**, "you have seen about 80% of what the game offers on the first floor", and weapons and power-ups that lack impact.
- **For Rack Farm:** a day timer that **kills extend** is a cheap way to give a day shape. Include **assist options** from the start. Three hours of one idea is thin; five weapons across four weeks should be paced to avoid the "first floor is 80%" trap.

**10. Nubby's Number Factory** (plinko roguelite with quotas)
- **Mine:** a **quota against a deadline** with a **comic threat** ("the sun will implode") at $4.99 and 19.8K reviews. It is the closest in spirit to **installed MW against the IPO threshold**.
- **Warning** [player report]: complaints are about **luck and physics randomness** ("you're at the mercy of shop and ball physics"), so players blame randomness for failing.
- **For Rack Farm:** make misses feel like the player's mistakes, not dice. Keep the threshold deterministic and the creatures' paths readable.

## What this says about Rack Farm [inference]
1. **Make plugged-in creatures resist weapons.** Orcs Must Die! 3 and Sanctum 2 both slid into plain shooters because weapons beat the tower-side tools. Your room tricks only matter if weapons barely work on plugged creatures.
2. **Role clarity beats variety.** Five weapons with unmistakable roles and a cue on each target (ULTRAKILL, DOOM, Plants vs. Zombies) is enough, as the owner said.
3. **A new creature type with a new answer on a steady schedule** is Plants vs. Zombies' whole engine, at $4.99 and 98%. It fits "two new things a week".
4. **Every purchase must be felt next day.** Roboquest's complaints ("minuscule" upgrades, a grind before anything matters) are the same as Zero Stress King.
5. **The vacuum works if capacity is generous.** Slime Rancher's pain is the tiny inventory, not the tool.
6. **Cute plus scheming** is right: people feel guilt about cute victims (Slime Rancher), and the joke is what made Can 'Em All pop.
7. **Avoid forced returns on a timer** (Dome Keeper) and **avoid luck-driven failure** (Nubby's).
8. **Offer assist options early** (Mullet MadJack's clock).
9. **Say what each update changed** (Dome Keeper's "no in-game explanation").

## Limits
The review samples are the most-voted, so they favor strong opinions and jokes. For ULTRAKILL and DOOM Eternal that left almost no design commentary, so those two entries lean on the store text and general knowledge. Nothing was played. A Steam search cannot show how each game feels, so the MVP should test the weapon-versus-trick question first.
