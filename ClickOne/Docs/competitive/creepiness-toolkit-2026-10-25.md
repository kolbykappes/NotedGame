# Creepiness toolkit for Noted (synthesis of 20 summary scans)

*2026-10-25. Source: five scan files (`creepiness-scan-2026-10-25-a` to `-e`), 20 games, public data only, nothing played. Several sources failed to load (Reddit tool timeout, Polygon, Slant, TV Tropes, the Lobotomy Corp wiki, the Paperclips wiki and GDC deck); claims tagged [inference] in the scan files need checking before we rely on them. Evidence is thinnest for Papers, Please day detail, Lethal Company scare moments, Observation Duty 1 and Lobotomy Corp.*

## The one idea all five groups agree on

**Keep the verbs fixed and change what they touch.** Fishing, newscasting, card duels, paperclip making and cookie clicking never change their controls; the content, labels, logs and consequences mutate. For Noted: the eleven jobs keep their shape for the whole run, and the weirdness lives in the text, props, logs and rules around them.

## Principles (with where each comes from)

1. **Teach a boring baseline, then delta it.** Exit 8 and Observation Duty work because the player has memorised an ugly, plain space. Wrongness is cheap when it is a change to an existing asset (scale, text, count, light colour, position, a date a day ahead), not a new model.
2. **Two registers.** Many small deniable oddities plus rare unmistakable ones. Both are needed (Exit 8, Observation Duty).
3. **Plain statement, impossible content.** Stanley, Control, Lobotomy Corp and Kentucky Route Zero all state the wrong thing in the same flat voice as the right thing. Recipe: passive voice, reference numbers, routine verbs, no adjective that tells you to be scared, one wrong item among ordinary ones, never labelled odd.
4. **The mundane UI is the delivery surface.** Rule sheets, logs, task lists, tickers, terminals. When a tool you trust lies, dread follows (Mouthwashing's task list, Papers Please's contradicting rule sheet, Paperclips' log).
5. **Evidence of people, never people.** A prior user implied by a tool description, a drive labelled for someone absent, a note from "former trainees". A repeated named presence seen only through traces creates attachment more cheaply than a character (B, D, A all say this; it fits our rule that nobody appears).
6. **Delay and persistence turn a scare into dread.** A consequence slip arrives the next day (Papers Please); something broken on day 4 is still broken on day 9 (DDLC, Inscryption, Dredge).
7. **Wrongness that pays.** Dredge aberrations earn more; Not For Broadcast complicity earns pay. The player cooperates with the creepiness. Tie odd items to higher coin value.
8. **Legible rules beat random scares.** Dredge's panic and light rules, Lobotomy Corp's handling rules: the player can read the system and fear it. A visible late-shift rule is better than a jump scare.
9. **Stages, not a ramp.** Each stage changes one or two things and stays stable long enough to feel normal (Cookie Clicker, Paperclips).
10. **Stay in one room-set and one loop.** Inscryption's Acts 2 and 3 and DDLC after the reveal are the cautionary tales: switching form costs mood.

## Pacing target (inference, not measured)

Clean stretch before the first plainly-off thing: DDLC is about 40-50% of a short core, Dredge under 10% of a long run. A safe target for 5-6 hours is roughly **10-15% clean (days 1-2)**, then one plainly-off thing, then deniable escalation. How many oddities: Exit 8 reportedly has about 30; one Observation Duty 5 map lists about 50 across 12 categories. **Inference: 20-30 distinct oddities in 8-10 families, each family varied across rooms, is enough to feel solid.**

## Things to avoid (from the scans)

- Genre or form switches midway (Inscryption Acts 2-3).
- Telling the player to be scared: adjectives, dramatic music cues on text.
- Anything that reaches outside the game (Pony Island style) beyond fake in-game error text.
- Full characters or recurring figures on screen (rule: nobody appears).
- Random scares with no readable rule behind them.
