# What felt impactful: Bills Must Be Paid, Birb, Sludgineers, Dwarf Eats Mountain

*2026-10-21. Research pass on four games the owner says "felt more impactful" than Zero Stress King. Nothing here was played by Claude. Sources: Steam store text, the 7 most-helpful positive and 5 most-helpful negative English Steam reviews per game, Reddit search results (post text, not comment trees). Evidence tags: **[store]**, **[player report]**, **[dev]** (developer's own words), **[inference]**.*

## What the owner said (2026-10-21, verbatim)
> I think a hybrid between full on idler with some targeting keeps things the most interesting. The game can be MUCH shorter - I think 4-5 hours if fine for a $5 game.
>
> Some recent games that felt more impactful when I played them: Bills to be paid, Birb, Sludgineers, Dwarf Eats Mountain

This turns the concept into: **an idler with some targeting (not targeting-only), 4 to 5 hours, about $5.**

## 1. Bills Must Be Paid (already in [short-incrementals-what-works.md](short-incrementals-what-works.md))
- **Loop** [store]: smashing piggy banks costs **stamina**; when your hand tires, **the run ends**. **Bills** arrive; ignore them and you go bankrupt. Paying bills unlocks perks and "legacy points" for rings and bracelets you keep after bankruptcy. Piggies run away, are lazy, or carry big loot. A skill tree, different hammers (crit vs. radius vs. speed), abilities such as "rock rain".
- **What players say it does** [player report]: "very high intensity fun for about 3 to 5 hours", "extremely satisfying BREAKING piggy banks", "nothing idle about this game", "Whack-A-Mole meets Vampire Survivors", "a level of strategy in knowing when to upgrade and when to pay bills".
- **Where it falls short** [player report]: **cost scaling means "all roads lead to Rome"**, a win in about four runs regardless of skill; every run is "30 seconds of fun, then 30 seconds of aim trainer"; "wonky targeting"; "if you don't like it in 5 minutes it will not change".
- **Why it may feel impactful** [inference]: a **tiny, intense, stamina-timed run**, a clear threat (bills), and **violent visible feedback on every hit**. The weakness is the same as the owner's: the tree does not matter much, but the **moment-to-moment hit** carries the game.

## 2. Birb
- **Loop** [store]: eat popcorn to **mutate into stronger forms** (evolutions); step through a portal into **procedural dungeons** with bosses; a **fishing minigame** with a "Fishdex" and aquarium; a nest with passive bonuses, **automation** and companions; **other players move in real time in the park**, chat, and leaderboards. "Satisfying Idle progression", "Active RPG-style combat expeditions", "Hundreds of upgrades and skill trees".
- **What players say** [player report]: "first 20 to 25 hours there's always something new and fun", "more of this type of idle game is needed", "for 5 bucks it's an amazing value", things become automated with each evolution.
- **Where it falls apart** [player report]: after **evolution 4 or 5, floor 9** it becomes **"a slog"**: "upgrades give you 1% and enemies get a 20x increase"; **"standing AFK for 50 hours"**; a weak Steam web wrapper (RAM use, Steam Deck). The 85% rating comes from this late-game stall.
- **Reddit** [dev, player report]: started as a free itch.io game with weekly updates; the developer hit about 3,000 daily players and a 1.7K-member Discord within two months. It is ranked **C tier** on one 88-game list.
- **Why it may feel impactful** [inference]: **each evolution visibly changes what you are**, and each stage **unlocks and automates a new activity**. It is the **opposite length** of what the owner wants (hundreds of hours), so the lesson is the early stretch, not the whole game.

## 3. Sludgineers
- **Loop** [store]: "clean, mine and refine pollution". You drive a rig through the countryside, **suck up sludge, jackhammer ore, filter gas, kill volcanoes**, earn cash and resources, build a **refinery**, upgrade equipment, unlock areas, complete quests. Drones help later. Tags: Cozy, Cleaning, Relaxing.
- **What players say** [player report]: "like PowerWash", **"the satisfaction of cleaning out the world and upgrading yourself as you go"**, "watching it get restored", levels that **introduce new mechanics one at a time**, "went non-verbal and 3.5 hours had passed".
- **Where it falls short** [player report]: **short** (3 to 5 hours, 13 levels); **you replay the last level for cash** to finish the tree (about half the tree done at the end for some); "the incremental portion doesn't make you feel stronger unless you go back and replay old levels"; refined-resource bottleneck; late stutter.
- **Developers** [dev]: **two people, one year**; $6.99 with a 20% launch discount; about **4 hours main + 1.5 hours completionist**; patched within days of launch (late-game grind rebalance). Route: itch.io demo, Steam demo, a big creator-email campaign, a festival bundle, Next Fest.
- **Why it may feel impactful** [inference]: **the world visibly changes as you work** (dirty to clean), the rig has a **physical, aimed verb**, and mechanics arrive gradually. Its size and price **match the owner's target** (4 to 5 hours, about $5).

## 4. Dwarf Eats Mountain
- **Loop** [store]: a **strategic incremental** about devouring mountains for gold. Hire dwarves, deploy **war machines** (dynamite throwers, ballistas, flamers, lasers), collect **artifacts** to build synergies and grow each run. **Runners haul the gold home** weaving through falling rocks; the more damage the mountain takes, the more **calamities** it unleashes. Roguelite runs and ascensions.
- **Reddit** [dev]: solo developer; the early-game was sped up after feedback, and **an active "click the ore to nudge it toward the stash" verb was added** "to give something active to do during downtime". Players run strategies that go **idle for hours**.
- **What players say** [player report]: addictive ("played this for 3 days straight"), "the first real contender to Gnorp", a power curve with "a couple of balancing factors that keep it interesting"; the Steam Deck port works well.
- **Where it falls short** [player report]: **"not worth $10"** for an incremental; **synergies not shown** ("the DPM meter doesn't break it out"); "you don't feel the power crawl"; "only 1 way to play"; prestige choices are **not refundable**; runners being knocked out is frustrating; "boring progression" for some. Rated 81%.
- **Why it may feel impactful** [inference]: **a physical, readable world (the mountain being eaten)** with **hazards that fight back**, plus a small active verb. Several complaints (synergy not visible, no feeling of power) are the **same as the owner's** about Zero Stress King, so the owner's good experience may come from the first runs.

## What the four have in common [inference]
1. **The world changes where you can see it.** Bills: piggies shatter; Birb: the creature evolves; Sludgineers: the land turns clean; Dwarf: the mountain is eaten. Zero Stress King shows only a flood and a counter.
2. **There is something that pushes back or a clock.** Bills: stamina and bills; Dwarf: calamities and falling rocks; Sludgineers: levels; Birb: dungeon floors. (Zero Stress King has lava and nothing at stake.)
3. **A direct verb for the player's hand**, but in **short bursts** with **automation** around it. This is the owner's "hybrid idler with some targeting".
4. **Late-game collapse is the common complaint**: Birb's floor-9 slog, Sludgineers and Bills' replay-for-grind, Dwarf's bland synergies. The owner's 4 to 5 hours avoids most of it.
5. **Price and length:** the best-received are **$5 to $7 and 3 to 5 hours**; Dwarf at $9.99 draws "not worth it". Matches the owner's $5 and 4 to 5 hour target.

## What this suggests for ClickOne (hypotheses)
- **Make the board show the progress**: something that visibly grows, cleans, or evolves as the player upgrades.
- **Give each run a clock or a threat** that is not a loss: a stamina or timer ends the run (Bills), and the result only changes the pay.
- **Keep targeting short and bounded** (a few seconds at a time) and let **automation** carry the rest, so the opt-out is genuinely playable. (Compare Zero Stress King's paid auto-aim, which the owner called "super underwhelming".)
- **Introduce one new mechanic per stage** (Sludgineers) and **stop at 4 to 5 hours**.
- **Show what each upgrade did**, e.g. a synergy breakdown, to avoid Dwarf Eats Mountain's complaint.
