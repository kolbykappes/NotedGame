# Discovery pass 2026-10-21: single-screen, aim-optional, no-fail incrementals

*Run 2026-10-21 (Steam data as of that day). Question from the owner: find similar incremental games on Steam, especially (a) one screen,
(b) your input is aiming, and you may skip aiming at some efficiency cost, (c) DNA-style spending, and (d) **no way to lose**: has anyone else done

even stronger", *Incrempire*; "The tower will inevitably fall", *BastionOS*). So no-fail is a **small, real sub-niche**, not the norm. I cannot
prove nobody else has done it: see limits below.

### What the best precedent teaches: Zero Stress King (all 117 English negatives read, 887 positives scanned by keyword)

*Superseded in detail by the full deep dive: [02-zero-stress-king.md](02-zero-stress-king.md), which adds the developer posts, review timeline, language mix and a sales estimate.*

- **No-fail did not hurt it.** Only about 4 of 117 negatives (keyword match on challenge, tension, lose, risk; approximate) talk about the
  missing challenge. Compare ShredDead's demo threads, where it is the main argument. One difference: Zero Stress King's store page sold it
  as "zero stress" from the first line, so the players who bought it wanted that.
- **What the negatives actually complain about** (keyword matches, approximate, out of 117): too short, about 29; boring or repetitive, about
  30; shallow or samey upgrades, about 20; "nothing to do / just idle / screensaver", about 33; price or "not worth it", about 19; balance or pacing,
  about 16; bugs, about 11; AI, 1.
- **Length is the weak point.** Many finish in 5 to 8 hours ("you prestige once and pick the OP ones and its GG"); several say it is too
  short at $5 to $7. The median negative reviewer had 8.7 hours; the median positive reviewer had 13.1 hours. The prestige layer is called
  "superfluous" because you can finish without it.
- **Top negative review (100 votes, 0 hours):** unremovable pop-up ads for other games in the options menus of a paid game. Players hate this
  more than anything about the design.
- **Save wipes and unreachable achievements** show up repeatedly (several reviewers say a patch reset their save; two cannot reach 100%).
- **It also has enemy upgrades.** Two negatives mention "increasing enemy XP, health and dmg" and "upgrading enemy using skills". The "you buy
  harder enemies" economy is therefore not unique to ShredDead.
- **Good news:** "Zero stress" is the one thing positive reviewers repeat: "perfect to play and watch a show on your other monitor", "Truly zero
  stress. Satisfying to see numbers go up and enemies explode", "polish: sound, art, feedback, 0 lag" (even a negative review praised it).
- **Format:** single screen (store screenshot shows a left shop panel, a lava column and a pixel horde on one screen). No aiming: it is a pure
  idler apart from a later "left click to supercharge your monument" button that one reviewer called a chore.

*Inference:* the lesson for a no-fail game is that a clear promise and polish carry it, but it needs more than 5 to 8 hours of real decisions,
and players punish ads, save loss and fake prestige.

## Observations across the field (single-source, provisional)

1. **Price and length cluster:** most successful peers sit between $1 and $7 and last about 4 to 8 hours; Orc Problem at $9.99 shows the top end.
   "Short" is both the selling point (Outhold, Soba's Gunpaw state it) and the top complaint when it falls under the price.
2. **Review scores are high for the genre:** Very Positive is normal for the focused short ones (86% to 97%); the 60% to 70% group tends to be
   unfocused or long-grind titles.
3. **The enemy-buff economy appears in at least two games** (ShredDead, Zero Stress King), so it is a recognisable device, not a unique one.
4. **Desktop-companion framing is common** (Desktop Defender, Desktop Tower Survivor, Tiny Ring Defense, Wartide): small always-on windows. A
   single-screen game that works in that format has a built-in audience.
5. **Art varies from placeholder to painted.** The unreleased zombie games (Wipe Them Out!, Horde Below) have the strongest art of those viewed;
   Zero Stress King shows that readable pixel art with strong feedback is enough.

## Method and limits

- **Tag IDs:** Incremental 560542, Idler 615955, Auto Battler 1084988, Tower Defense 1645, Top-Down Shooter 4637, Bullet Hell 4885, Shoot 'Em Up
  4255, Shooter 1774, Isometric 5851, Relaxing 1654, Sci-fi 3942, Space 1755, Fantasy 1684, Loot 4236. Source: `store.steampowered.com/tagdata/populartags/english`.
- **Queries (`tools/steam.mjs find`):** 20 AND-combinations of the above on released games (up to 300 to 400 results each, ordered by review count, plus
  newest-first for the Auto Battler and Tower Defense pairs), and 16 on unreleased games (`filter=comingsoon`). This produced 1,972 unique released
  and 839 unique unreleased games. The pool is capped, so a game with few reviews and weak tag overlap can be missing. The store reports 8,925 released
  and 1,549 unreleased titles with the Incremental tag alone, so this is a sample weighted to the closest tag overlaps.
- **Ranking:** a simple score of how many combinations a game appeared in, weighted toward Auto Battler, Tower Defense and shooter tags. Then 77 games
  chosen by judgment were enriched with description, user tags and review summary.
- **Fail-state check:** I scanned up to 300 English reviews each (fewer where a game has few) for 33 released games with keywords such as "can't
  lose", "no fail", "no game over", plus their full store text. Store text search for phrases such as "no lose condition" is not usable: Steam search is fuzzy and
  returned unrelated games. Absence of a keyword hit is **not** evidence that a game can be lost.
- **Not done:** no game was played; community threads were not read for peers; the Steam search `term` and SteamSpy were tried and not relied on
  (SteamSpy's tag listing returned unrelated games); itch.io and non-Steam stores were not searched.
- **Reproduce:** `node tools/steam.mjs find 560542,1084988 300 Reviews_DESC` and `... find 560542,1645 300 - comingsoon`.
